Historic Theater Reopens After $12 Million Renovation Project

The Grand Majestic, built in 1923, restored to original Art Deco splendor

The Grand Majestic Theater, a landmark building that has graced downtown for over a century, officially reopened its doors last Friday after an extensive $12 million restoration project. The theater, which closed in 2018 due to structural concerns and declining attendance, has been meticulously restored to its original 1923 Art Deco glory.

Hundreds of community members gathered for the grand reopening ceremony, which featured a ribbon-cutting by the mayor and a special performance by the city's symphony orchestra. The event marked the culmination of a three-year fundraising and restoration effort led by the Historic Preservation Society.

"This theater is more than just a building—it's a piece of our city's cultural heritage," said preservation society director Amanda Foster. "We've brought back the original chandeliers, restored the intricate plasterwork, and even recreated the original velvet curtains that were lost decades ago."

The renovation included modern updates while preserving historical character. The original 1,200-seat auditorium now features state-of-the-art sound and lighting systems hidden behind restored period-appropriate fixtures. Accessibility improvements include wheelchair ramps and an elevator, all designed to blend seamlessly with the Art Deco aesthetic.

Funding came from a combination of private donations, grants from the National Endowment for the Arts, and a bond measure approved by voters. Local businesses contributed significantly, recognizing the theater's potential to revitalize the downtown entertainment district.

The theater's first season includes a mix of classic films, live theater productions, and musical performances. Tickets for the opening month sold out within 48 hours of going on sale, demonstrating strong community support for the restored venue.

Architectural historians praised the restoration work, noting that the project serves as a model for preserving historic buildings while adapting them for modern use. The Grand Majestic is now listed on the National Register of Historic Places.
